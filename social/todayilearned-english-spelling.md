# r/todayilearned

**Title:** TIL the silent B in "debt" was never pronounced — Renaissance scholars added it to make the word look more Latin (it came from French "dette")

**Body:**

English got "debt" from Old French "dette" and "doubt" from "doute." No B in either one. But scholars in the 1500s decided the spellings should show off their Latin roots ("debitum," "dubitare"), so they stuck the B in. Nobody ever said it out loud.

Same deal with "island" — they assumed it was from Latin "insula" and wedged in an S. It's actually from Old English "igland" (water-land). The S was just wrong.

"Receipt" got a phantom P from Latin "recepta." "Scissors" picked up a C from Latin "scissor" even though it came through French "cisoires."

Meanwhile other silent letters used to be real sounds — the K in "knife" and the W in "write" were both pronounced until a few hundred years ago. English spelling is basically a museum of accidents.
