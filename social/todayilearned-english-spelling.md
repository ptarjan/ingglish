# r/todayilearned

**Title:** TIL the silent B in "debt" and "doubt" was never pronounced in English — scholars in the 1500s added it to make the words look more Latin

**Body:**

English borrowed "debt" from Old French "dette" and "doubt" from "doute." Neither had a B. But Renaissance scholars thought the words should reflect their Latin ancestors ("debitum" and "dubitare"), so they inserted the B into the spelling. The pronunciation never changed.

The same thing happened to "island" — scholars assumed it came from Latin "insula" and added a silent S. It actually comes from Old English "igland" (literally "water-land"). The S was wrong from the start.

"Receipt" got a silent P for the same reason (Latin "recepta"), and "scissors" got a C (Latin "scissor," but the English word came through Old French "cisoires").

About 60% of English words contain at least one silent letter. Some used to be pronounced (the K in "knife," the W in "write"), but others were never said out loud — they were added by people who thought English should look more like Latin.
